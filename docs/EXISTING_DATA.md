# Existing data on tech abuse in Mississippi, the South and rural areas

`docs/DECISIONS.md` listed an open question: is comparable Southern or rural tech-abuse data already published? This is a first pass at answering it, done on 2026-09-26. It is not a systematic review.

## Bottom line

I found **no published source of advocate-reported, recurring tech-abuse data for Mississippi, the Deep South, or rural areas below the state level.** That's the kind of data this project would produce. What does exist:

- **Population surveys** of how many people experience stalking or some forms of tech abuse. They're national, and a few break results down by state, Census region or rural/urban. They measure victimization in the general population, not what survivors bring to advocates.
- **National surveys of advocates** about tech abuse. The ones I found report national figures only.
- **Studies of rural domestic violence** that aren't about technology, and studies of technology-facilitated abuse (TFA) that aren't about rural areas or the South.

So the gap looks real, but read it as "not found in this search", not "doesn't exist". The limits below matter, and some organizations may hold relevant data they haven't published.

## How this was checked, and its limits

- **Sources searched:** web search, and PubMed through its connector.
- **Not read in full:** this environment's network policy blocked direct access to nnedv.org, cdc.gov, icpsr.umich.edu, ojp.gov and ncbi.nlm.nih.gov. For those sources I saw only search-result excerpts.
- **Labels used below:**
  - **Read** means the PubMed record, including the abstract, was read.
  - **Excerpt** means the source was found by search but not opened, so any figure quoted from it comes from a search excerpt. **Check excerpt figures against the source before quoting them anywhere.**
- **Not searched systematically:** computer-science venues (CHI, USENIX Security, CSCW), where much tech-abuse clinic research is published; criminology databases; and state agency data portals.
- **Unpublished data:** coalitions and national organizations may hold data they don't publish. Only asking them will tell.

### Research literature

According to PubMed, these searches found no tech-abuse study set in Mississippi or the Deep South:
- tech abuse or cyberstalking, combined with intimate partner violence, combined with Mississippi, Alabama, Louisiana, Arkansas, "Deep South" or "southern United States";
- rural or nonmetropolitan, combined with tech abuse, combined with partner or domestic violence.

Relevant records that were read:

- Rogers et al., 2022, *Technology-Facilitated Abuse in Intimate Relationships: A Scoping Review*, Trauma, Violence, & Abuse. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/15248380221090218). Read (abstract only). It covers 22 studies from 2000 to 2020 across the UK, Ireland, USA, Canada, New Zealand and Australia. The abstract doesn't mention rural or Southern US settings.
- Peek-Asa et al., 2011, *Rural disparity in domestic violence prevalence and access to resources*, Journal of Women's Health. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1089/jwh.2011.2891). Read. An Iowa clinic survey. Women in small rural and isolated areas reported more intimate partner violence and lived much farther from services. It isn't about technology.
- Peitzmeier et al., 2021, *Increases in Intimate Partner Violence During COVID-19: Prevalence and Correlates*, Journal of Interpersonal Violence. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/08862605211052586). Read. A Michigan survey that included tech-facilitated partner violence, with quota sampling matched to the state's urban/rural mix. It isn't Southern.
- Livings et al., 2026, *Technology-Facilitated Intimate Partner Violence Among U.S. Young Adults: Associations With Physical Intimate Partner Violence, Gender Identity, and Mental Health Outcomes*, Journal of Interpersonal Violence. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/08862605261426588). Read. National data on young adults from the Future of Families and Child Wellbeing Study, with no regional focus.

### Services and tools: context, not data

- **Tech abuse clinics.** The clinics named in search results were the Clinic to End Tech Abuse ([CETA](https://ceta.tech.cornell.edu/resources), NYC), the Madison Tech Clinic, UC Berkeley's CLTC, and Seattle's TECC. None in the South turned up. That's one search, not a census. A [Technology Abuse Clinic Toolkit](https://www.techabuseclinics.org/) exists for starting new clinics.
- **Sherloc** ([GitHub](https://github.com/sophiestephenson/sherloc)): per its repository description, a tool for collecting evidence of intimate partner surveillance for legal proceedings, adapted from the ISDi spyware-discovery tool. The spec names ISDi and Sherloc output as sources for this project's `finding_detail` and `platform` lists.
- **Federal funding:** [OVC FY25 Services for Victims of Technology-Facilitated Abuse](https://ovc.ojp.gov/funding/opportunities/o-ovc-2025-172431). Excerpt. Per the excerpts, it funded services for tech-abuse victims in response to the TAKE IT DOWN Act, nonprofits were eligible, awards were up to $1,000,000, and the deadline was August 2025, so it's closed. I don't know whether there's an FY26 round.

## What this means for this project

1. **Position it as complementing the population surveys, not duplicating them.** Population surveys (NISVS, NCHS) say how common victimization is. Nothing found says what survivors in Mississippi bring to advocates, where, and how that changes over time. That's the gap this project fills. The accurate claim is "no advocate-reported, regional, recurring tech-abuse data was found", not "no data exists".
2. **Few programs means a region's numbers could reveal one program.** If Mississippi has about 10 domestic violence programs (per the DV Counts excerpt), some regions may be served by a single program. That region's counts would then be that program's caseload, which is a disclosure about an organization, not a person. The current privacy rules don't cover it. It should shape the region definitions (see `docs/DECISIONS.md`).
3. **Expect heavy suppression.** Data from a handful of programs, split by quarter, region and abuse type, will produce many small counts. Coarser regions or longer periods may be needed. That's a design question for the partner and the statistician review, not something to guess at now.
4. **Match categories to NISVS where possible.** If the `finding_detail` and `platform` lists line up with NISVS's tech-facilitated stalking tactics, this project's numbers can be read next to the state estimates. The final lists still need advocate input.
